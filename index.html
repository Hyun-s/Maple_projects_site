Maple Research: Applied Generative Synthesis & Grounded Knowledge Systems

A unified research portfolio connecting generative character synthesis and evidence-grounded retrieval systems.

Every project publishes verifiable benchmarks, failure post-mortems, and inspectable technical records.

Abstract

Modern applied machine-learning systems operating in stylized gaming environments face two fundamental challenges: faithfully capturing fine-grained visual identity in generative diffusion models, and preventing hallucination in conversational retrieval systems. This portfolio presents two complementary systems built end-to-end: (1) Maple Character Translation, an experimental framework evaluating continuous latent adaptation (LoRA) versus discrete token optimization (Textual Inversion) across SDXL and FLUX.1-dev architectures, including a 90,000-step failure analysis on compositional equipment binding; and (2) Maple Chat, an Agent-first assistant combining BGE-M3 dense retrieval, lexical search, BGE-Reranker scoring, PostgreSQL knowledge relations, and route-specific attribution and output checks.


Character Translation & Identity Inversion

Can text-to-image diffusion models learn the visual identity of individual 2D pixel-art cosmetic items and recombine them arbitrarily into novel high-quality character illustrations? We benchmark adaptation methods across a controlled 10 hats × 10 tops × 10 bottoms dataset (1,000 outfit combinations).

Key Empirical Finding. While LoRA rank-16 achieves strong distributional fidelity on character translation, naive embedding-only textual inversion fails to bind item identities even after 90,000 optimization steps. Detailed spatial analysis shows that sprite foreground occupies only 2.73% of the canvas, causing whole-image MSE loss to focus on blank background rather than equipment details.

Evidence-Grounded Conversational RAG

A local-first Discord assistant with an Agent tool loop and an alternative QA path. BGE-M3 dense retrieval and lexical search are fused with RRF, reranked with BGE-Reranker-v2-m3, and supplemented by reviewed entity relations stored in PostgreSQL.

Inspectable Service Record. The updated study separates service requirements, model preprocessing and postprocessing, retrieval evidence, graph relations, and known implementation limits. It includes real Korean chatbot runs with observed tool traces rather than unsupported accuracy claims.

Engineering & Disclosure Principles

1. State the Evidence: Every claim carries a dated snapshot, explicit scope, and reproducible benchmark rather than informal assertions.

2. Show the Boundary: Public architectures, metrics, and methodology remain completely decoupled from private credentials, live endpoints, and confidential corpora.

3. Keep Failures Visible: Negative optimization results (such as 90k FLUX drift) are treated as first-class scientific findings, accompanied by failure mechanisms and regression tests.

4. Prefer Static Records: Technical reports, visual inspection grids, and metrics remain fast, deterministic, and auditable without runtime backend dependencies.
