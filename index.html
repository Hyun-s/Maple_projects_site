Abstract

Modern applied machine-learning systems operating in stylized gaming environments face two fundamental challenges: faithfully capturing fine-grained visual identity in generative diffusion models, and preventing hallucination in conversational retrieval systems. This portfolio presents two complementary systems built end-to-end: (1) Maple Character Translation, an experimental framework evaluating continuous latent adaptation (LoRA) versus discrete token optimization (Textual Inversion) across SDXL and FLUX.1-dev architectures, including a 90,000-step failure analysis on compositional equipment binding; and (2) Maple Chat, a hybrid information-retrieval pipeline combining dense semantic embeddings, lexical BM25 matching, cross-encoder reranking, and explicit knowledge graph entity grounding with complete source provenance.


Character Translation & Identity Inversion

Can text-to-image diffusion models learn the visual identity of individual 2D pixel-art cosmetic items and recombine them arbitrarily into novel high-quality character illustrations? We benchmark adaptation methods across a controlled 10 hats × 10 tops × 10 bottoms dataset (1,000 outfit combinations).

Key Empirical Finding. While LoRA rank-16 achieves strong distributional fidelity on character translation, naive embedding-only textual inversion fails to bind item identities even after 90,000 optimization steps. Detailed spatial analysis shows that sprite foreground occupies only 2.73% of the canvas, causing whole-image MSE loss to focus on blank background rather than equipment details.

Evidence-Grounded Conversational RAG

A local-first conversational assistant deployed to resolve domain-specific gaming queries. Unifies dense semantic embeddings (BGE-large), lexical matching (BM25), cross-encoder reranking (bge-reranker-large), and structured Neo4j knowledge graph entity grounding.

Production Quality Loop. Evaluated across realistic user questions with strict grounding boundaries. Incorporates a continuous feedback loop driven by observed production failures (temporal patch drift, ambiguous item naming, and cross-source conflicts).

Engineering & Disclosure Principles

1. State the Evidence: Every claim carries a dated snapshot, explicit scope, and reproducible benchmark rather than informal assertions.

2. Show the Boundary: Public architectures, metrics, and methodology remain completely decoupled from private credentials, live endpoints, and confidential corpora.

3. Keep Failures Visible: Negative optimization results (such as 90k FLUX drift) are treated as first-class scientific findings, accompanied by failure mechanisms and regression tests.

4. Prefer Static Records: Technical reports, visual inspection grids, and metrics remain fast, deterministic, and auditable without runtime backend dependencies.
