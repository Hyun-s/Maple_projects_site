Why 90,000 Steps Did Not Recover Item Identity

This page preserves the complete 3,000-to-90,000-step trajectory, explains why the training loss and the desired visual behavior diverged, and defines a cheaper staged experiment that can falsify each proposed explanation. The qualitative audit reports no aggregate metric.

Primary conclusionThe audited samples show no visual evidence of successful equipment-identity inversion. Additional embedding-only steps are not the appropriate intervention.

01 / Empirical finding

Longer optimization did not recover item identity

The fixed qualitative panel shows no consistent movement toward the target hat, top, or bottom under either prompt condition. The result is therefore reported as a negative result rather than selecting a visually convenient checkpoint.

02 / Evidence and interpretation

Why the training objective and the desired behavior diverged

Measured evidence is separated from mechanistic interpretation. The run establishes failure under this protocol; it does not establish that FLUX cannot represent the domain.

03 / Revised experimental program

Separate domain acquisition from item binding

A second 90,000-step embedding sweep is not warranted. The next program introduces capacity and supervision in stages, with a visual gate after each stage.

  1. Stage 1

    Establish the Maple rendering prior

    Train a rank-16 FLUX transformer LoRA on all 1,000 foreground-normalized targets for 1,000 steps. Use a shared descriptive caption and keep both text encoders frozen.

    Pass gate: centered full-body sprite structure appears under the exact domain prompt.
  2. Stage 2

    Bind individual equipment identifiers

    Freeze the accepted domain adapter. Learn FP32 CLIP-L identifier vectors from category-isolated and category-balanced examples. Keep T5 frozen rather than optimizing BF16 T5 token rows.

    Pass gate: changing one identifier changes the intended region while other regions remain stable.
  3. Stage 3

    Low-rate joint refinement

    Jointly refine the item identifiers and a small attention adapter with a lower learning rate. Sample exact-caption and anime-style panels every 250 steps.

    Stop rule: terminate when the fixed visual panel ceases to improve; do not optimize to a predetermined 90k horizon.

What Stage 1 does not test.

The domain-LoRA pilot does not claim item-specific inversion. It is a prerequisite test: if FLUX cannot reproduce the normalized Maple image domain with additional transformer capacity, identifier learning should not proceed on this backbone and preprocessing.
