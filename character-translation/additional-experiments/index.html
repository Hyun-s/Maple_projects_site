Textual Inversion Under SDXL and FLUX for Compositional Outfit Generation

We test whether jointly optimized item-token embeddings recover unseen hat–top–bottom combinations. SDXL and FLUX.1-dev are evaluated on the same factorial renders, combination split, prompts, and sample seeds.

Main finding Changing the backbone from SDXL to FLUX does not recover item binding

Both learned models remain below the shuffled real-image control. FLUX increases pixel similarity but reduces foreground IoU and CLIP similarity and increases LPIPS relative to SDXL. The qualitative outputs confirm that the pixel gain is dominated by the shared white background rather than the requested outfit identities.

01 / Experimental setup

Factorial rendering with a combination-level holdout

A pinned MapleStory.IO KMS snapshot supplies ten hats, ten tops, and ten bottoms. We render the complete 10×10×10 Cartesian product while holding character identity, pose, canvas, and background fixed. The exact rendered files and split are reused for both backbones.

03 / Evaluation

FLUX degrades semantic and foreground correspondence

The first 100 held-out combinations are generated with 30 inference steps. SDXL uses CFG 7.0 and its registered negative prompt; FLUX uses guidance 3.5 and no negative prompt. Both use the same positive item-token prompt and sample seed for each row.

Positive control

Oracle exact match

Each target is compared with an identical copy. This is not a model output; it checks that pairing and metric implementations recover the expected upper bound.

Negative control

Shuffled real image

Each target is compared with a different real render selected by deterministic derangement. It measures similarity caused by the shared body, pose, and background.

Learned model

SDXL textual inversion

Thirty item embeddings are optimized across both SDXL CLIP encoders while the VAE, UNet, text-encoder backbones, and all other embedding rows remain frozen.

Learned model

FLUX textual inversion

The same 30 item identifiers are optimized in the FLUX CLIP-L and T5 embedding spaces while the transformer, VAE, text encoders, and all non-item rows remain frozen.

Decision rule. Evidence for item binding should exceed the shuffled real-image control on semantic and perceptual measures and move toward the oracle upper bound. Neither learned model satisfies this criterion. FLUX further lowers CLIP from 0.5809 to 0.4537 and foreground IoU from 0.1793 to 0.0631 while increasing LPIPS from 0.3641 to 0.4332.

04 / Discussion

Embedding-only adaptation is insufficient in both backbones

The completed runs separate pipeline validity from scientific success. Token loading, held-out generation, target matching, and metric emission are correct, but neither backbone binds the requested hat, top, and bottom reliably. FLUX frequently leaves the MapleStory sprite domain entirely.

  1. 01

    Add category-isolated paired targets and renderer-derived item masks before further model comparisons.

  2. 02

    Report category retrieval accuracy in addition to whole-image CLIP, LPIPS, and foreground IoU.

  3. 03

    Evaluate domain LoRA followed by frozen-domain textual inversion to separate domain adaptation from identifier binding.

  4. 04

    Compare learned identifiers plus LoRA against embedding-only adaptation under the same split, prompts, and seeds.

Evaluation limitation. CLIP and LPIPS are computed over the complete normalized image, without item-specific masks. Category-conditioned summaries are therefore not localized garment measurements.
