Jointly optimizing 30 item-token embeddings under SDXL and FLUX.1-dev on 1,000 factorial renders.
Anime-style prompting alters stylistic appearance but fails to recover individual item identity.

Abstract & Findings

We test whether jointly optimized item-token embeddings recover unseen hat–top–bottom combinations. A pinned MapleStory KMS renderer supplies the complete Cartesian product of 10 hats, 10 tops, and 10 bottoms (1,000 distinct outfits). We evaluate embedding-only textual inversion across SDXL 1.0 and FLUX.1-dev, and test whether anime-domain prompt interventions (an anime-style character wearing {tokens}) can repair item-token binding.

Conclusive Finding Prompt wording alone does not repair failed item binding

The newly sampled outputs are recognizable anime characters rather than icons or blank frames, but they do not reproduce the requested hat, top, and bottom. CLIP similarity changes only marginally from 0.4537 to 0.4642, while foreground IoU drops to 0.0503 and LPIPS rises to 0.5015. Prompt engineering cannot compensate for representational and spatial capacity mismatch.

Discussion & Next Steps

The prompt intervention demonstrates that text conditioning can steer the aesthetic output domain toward stylized anime rendering. However, because token embeddings failed to capture fine-grained pixel item semantics during training, the resulting character outfits are generic substitutes rather than the specified items.

  1. Foreground Scaling: Sprites must be cropped and enlarged to at least 75% canvas occupancy prior to optimization.
  2. Pre-training Domain LoRA: The base transformer must acquire the 2D pixel-art domain with rank-16 LoRA before attempting identity token binding.
  3. Localized Item Masks: Three-item joint captions should be supplemented with category-isolated pairs and segmentation masks.
